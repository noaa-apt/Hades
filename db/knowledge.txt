# Human Body & Health
what is the heart: Muscular organ that pumps blood through the body.
what are the lungs: Organs used for breathing; they exchange oxygen and carbon dioxide.
what is the brain: Organ that controls thought, memory, emotion and body functions; about 86 billion neurons.
what is a neuron: Nerve cell that transmits electrical and chemical signals.
what is the liver: Large organ that filters blood, makes bile and stores nutrients.
what is the pancreas: Organ that makes digestive enzymes and insulin.
what are the kidneys: Organs that filter blood and produce urine.
what is the spleen: Organ that filters blood and helps the immune system.
what is the thyroid: Gland in the neck that regulates metabolism.
what is insulin: Hormone that regulates blood sugar.
what is adrenaline: Hormone released in stress that prepares the body for fight or flight.
what is a hormone: Chemical messenger produced by glands that regulates body functions.
what is melatonin: Hormone that regulates the sleep-wake cycle.
what is serotonin: Neurotransmitter involved in mood, sleep and digestion.
what is dopamine: Neurotransmitter involved in reward, motivation and movement.
what is cortisol: Stress hormone released by the adrenal glands.
what is hemoglobin: Protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen.
what are red blood cells: Cells that carry oxygen from the lungs to the body.
what are white blood cells: Immune cells that fight infection.
what are platelets: Cell fragments that help blood clot.
what is plasma: Liquid part of blood, about 55 percent of its volume.
what is an antibody: Protein made by the immune system to neutralize pathogens.
what is a vaccine: Preparation that trains the immune system to recognize a disease.
what is an antibiotic: Medicine that kills or stops bacteria; it does not work against viruses.
what is the immune system: Network of cells and organs that defends the body against disease.
what is diabetes: Condition in which the body cannot properly regulate blood sugar.
what is cancer: Disease where abnormal cells grow uncontrollably.
what is a stroke: Interruption of blood supply to part of the brain.
what is a heart attack: Blockage of blood flow to part of the heart muscle.
what is high blood pressure: Condition where blood pushes too hard against artery walls; a reading of 130/80 or more is considered high by US guidelines.
what is normal blood pressure: Around 120/80 mmHg.
what is cholesterol: Waxy fat-like substance in blood; high LDL levels raise heart disease risk.
what is bmi: Body mass index, weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared.
what is a calorie: Unit of energy; a food calorie is 1,000 small calories (a kilocalorie).
how many calories should i eat a day: Roughly 2,000 for women and 2,500 for men on average, varying with age, size and activity.
how much water should i drink a day: Roughly 2 to 3 liters of total fluids daily for most adults, varying with size, climate and activity.
how much sleep do i need: Most adults need 7 to 9 hours per night.
what is rem sleep: Rapid eye movement sleep, the stage when most vivid dreaming occurs.
what is a vitamin: Organic compound the body needs in small amounts for normal function.
what is vitamin c: Antioxidant vitamin found in citrus fruit, peppers and berries; needed for collagen.
what is vitamin d: Vitamin made in skin from sunlight; helps calcium absorption and bone health.
what is vitamin b12: Vitamin needed for nerves and red blood cells; found in animal products.
what is vitamin a: Vitamin important for vision and immune function.
what is iron: Mineral needed to make hemoglobin; low iron causes anemia.
what is calcium: Mineral essential for bones, teeth, nerves and muscles.
what is protein: Nutrient made of amino acids that builds and repairs tissue.
what is a carbohydrate: Nutrient that is the body's main source of energy.

# More Science
what is chemistry: Science of matter, its properties and how it changes.
what is physics: Science of matter, energy, motion and forces.
what is biology: Science of living organisms.
what is geology: Science of Earth's structure, rocks and processes.
what is astronomy: Science of celestial objects and the universe.
what is meteorology: Science of weather and the atmosphere.
what is ecology: Study of how organisms interact with each other and their environment.
what is the scientific method: Process of observation, hypothesis, experiment, analysis and conclusion.
what is a hypothesis: Testable proposed explanation for an observation.
what is a theory in science: Well-tested explanation supported by large amounts of evidence.
what is newton's first law: An object stays at rest or in uniform motion unless acted on by a net force.
what is newton's second law: Force equals mass times acceleration (F = ma).
what is newton's third law: For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction.
what is the law of conservation of energy: Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed.
what is the first law of thermodynamics: Energy is conserved; the change in internal energy equals heat added minus work done.
what is the second law of thermodynamics: Entropy of an isolated system never decreases.
what is the theory of relativity: Einstein's theories of special relativity (1905) and general relativity (1915) describing space, time and gravity.
what is special relativity: Theory that the laws of physics are the same in all inertial frames and the speed of light is constant.
what is general relativity: Einstein's theory that gravity is the curvature of spacetime caused by mass and energy.
what is spacetime: Four-dimensional combination of three space dimensions and time.
what is time dilation: Effect in which time passes more slowly for objects moving fast or in strong gravity.
what is a wormhole: Hypothetical tunnel through spacetime connecting distant points.
what is string theory: Theoretical framework where particles are tiny vibrating strings.
what is the multiverse: Hypothetical set of multiple universes.
what is an electron: Negatively charged subatomic particle orbiting the nucleus.
what is a proton: Positively charged subatomic particle in the nucleus.
what is a neutron: Neutral subatomic particle in the nucleus.
what is a photon: Particle of light and carrier of electromagnetic force.
what is a neutrino: Nearly massless, neutral particle that rarely interacts with matter.
what is antimatter: Matter made of antiparticles with the opposite charge of normal particles.
what is the electromagnetic spectrum: Range of all electromagnetic radiation: radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray and gamma.
what is radio waves: Electromagnetic waves with the longest wavelengths, used in communication and broadcasting.
what is infrared: Electromagnetic radiation just beyond red light, felt as heat.
what is ultraviolet: Electromagnetic radiation beyond violet light; causes sunburn.
what is gamma radiation: Highest-energy electromagnetic radiation, from nuclear processes.
what is a laser: Device that emits a narrow coherent beam of light (Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation).
what is a semiconductor: Material whose conductivity lies between a conductor and an insulator, such as silicon.
what is a superconductor: Material that conducts electricity with zero resistance below a critical temperature.
what is voltage: Electrical potential difference measured in volts.
what is current: Flow of electric charge measured in amperes.
what is resistance: Opposition to current flow measured in ohms.
what is ohm's law: Voltage equals current times resistance (V = IR).
what is a watt: Unit of power equal to one joule per second.
what is a joule: Unit of energy.
what is a kilowatt hour: Energy equal to using 1,000 watts for one hour.
what is ac vs dc: Alternating current changes direction periodically; direct current flows one way.
what is a transistor: Semiconductor device that switches or amplifies signals; the building block of modern electronics.
what is moore's law: Observation that the number of transistors on a chip doubles about every two years.
what is magnetism: Force produced by moving electric charges, seen in magnets.
what is a magnetic field: Region around a magnet or current where magnetic force acts.
what is static electricity: Imbalance of electric charge on a surface.
what is friction: Force that resists the relative motion of surfaces in contact.
what is momentum: Mass times velocity.
what is kinetic energy: Energy of motion, one half mass times velocity squared.
what is potential energy: Stored energy due to position or condition.
what is work in physics: Force applied over a distance.
